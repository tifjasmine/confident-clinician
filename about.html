The Real Story

I know the "one more training will fix this" loop.

I have been the clinician buying the course, saving the post, searching the group thread, and opening another tab because I wanted to feel more prepared. Some trainings I finished. Some I started. Some simply helped me realize I was not the only one trying to make sense of the parts of clinical work that no one talks about clearly enough.

I have searched the questions newer therapists whisper to themselves: Is this normal? Why am I still thinking about that client? What do I do when the room goes quiet? How do I end on time without feeling cold? How do I market myself without feeling fake? How do I ask for support without sounding like I do not know what I am doing?

That search is not embarrassing. It is information. It means you care about the work and you are trying to become someone you can trust in the room.

What I Believe

You do not need to perform confidence to become confident.

Newer clinicians are often handed theory, ethics, acronyms, and a stack of models, then expected to somehow translate all of that into a living, breathing session with a real person in front of them.

I believe confidence grows when you have space to name what is actually happening, practice language that sounds like you, understand your own nervous system, and stop turning every hard moment into evidence that you are failing.

How I Work

Grounded, honest, practical, and deeply human.

Clinical Experience

I am a licensed professional counselor with experience across higher levels of care, crisis work, schools, community settings, private practice, and trauma-informed therapy spaces.

Body-Aware Lens

My work is shaped by nervous-system awareness, mindfulness, and body-based practices without claiming a title that does not fit the way I work now.

Real-Life Language

I care about language you can actually use when your mind goes blank, feedback stings, the room gets quiet, or your body starts bracing.

No Shame Spiral

This work is not about pretending you are above doubt. It is about learning how to come back to yourself without making uncertainty your enemy.

What I Am Here For

I want clinicians to feel less alone in the parts of the work that usually stay hidden.

  • the session you replay in your head on the drive home
  • the client silence that makes your brain sprint
  • the supervision question you are afraid sounds too basic
  • the note, boundary, cancellation, or feedback moment you keep overthinking
  • the quiet fear that everyone else knows what they are doing and you missed the memo

You are not the only one. And you do not have to build your clinical voice by guessing alone.

Also Me
