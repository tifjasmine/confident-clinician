The Real Story

I know the "one more training will fix this" loop.

I have been the clinician searching for one more training, one more course, one more explanation that would finally make the work feel less shaky. Some I finished. Some I started with the best intentions. All of it came from wanting to feel more prepared for the real moments that happen with real clients.

I have also been in the late-night search spiral, typing questions like, "Is this normal?" "Why am I still thinking about that session?" "What do I say when a client shuts down?" and "How do I market myself without feeling fake?" I created The Confident Clinician for therapists who are caring, capable, and still trying to find their footing in the parts of the work no one explains clearly enough.

Over the years, I've learned that many newer clinicians are not lacking passion, intelligence, or care. They're lacking a bridge.

A bridge between theory and real sessions.
A bridge between training and confidence.
A bridge between "I know the information" and "I know how to be with this person in front of me."

My work blends clinical clarity, nervous system awareness, self-trust, identity, and embodied presence - because the therapist is not separate from the work.

What I Believe

You do not need to perform confidence to become confident.

Newer clinicians are often handed theory, ethics, acronyms, and a stack of models, then expected to somehow translate all of that into a living, breathing session with a real person in front of them.

I believe confidence grows when you have space to name what is actually happening, practice language that sounds like you, understand your own nervous system, and stop turning every hard moment into evidence that you are failing.

How I Work

Compassionate, direct, and rooted in the whole self.

I do not believe therapy, mentorship, or professional development has to feel cold or overly clinical. I believe growth happens when you feel safe enough to be honest, supported enough to go deeper, and challenged enough to become clearer.

We look beneath the symptom.

Anxiety, burnout, self-criticism, people-pleasing, resentment, and disconnection often make more sense when we understand the story underneath them.

We include the body.

The body often holds what the mind has learned to explain away. Grounding, breath, awareness, movement, and nervous system support all have a place when they are useful.

We rebuild self-trust.

The work is about developing a relationship with yourself that feels less harsh, less performative, and more honest, compassionate, and rooted.

The therapist is not separate from the work. How you are in the room - your nervous system, your clarity, your willingness to be present - shapes everything that happens there.

My Practice

The Embodied Self

Virtual therapy in Pennsylvania & New Jersey

The Embodied Self is my private practice. I work with women who are tired of carrying everything alone - the anxiety, the burnout, the feeling that they should have it more together by now - and are ready to build a more honest, compassionate relationship with themselves.

I blend traditional talk therapy with body-based, mindfulness-informed approaches because I've seen how much healing gets missed when we only work from the neck up. The nervous system holds things. The body holds things. And sometimes the most important work happens when we finally slow down enough to notice.

You are not sitting across from a blank wall. You are working with a real person who brings warmth, creativity, groundedness, curiosity, and a little playfulness into the room.
