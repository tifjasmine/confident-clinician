Why I Created The Confident Clinician

Therapists need space to practice making decisions—not just more information to remember.

I kept noticing a gap between how much newer therapists were expected to know and how few opportunities they had to practice using that knowledge in context.

Real sessions do not arrive as clean case examples. Several concerns can matter at once. A response may not land the way you hoped. You may need to make a thoughtful decision before you feel completely certain.

Those moments require more than recalling a theory or finding the perfect intervention. They require you to notice what is happening, weigh your options, respond intentionally, and reflect without turning every imperfect moment into proof that you are unprepared.

I created The Confident Clinician to support that part of professional development. It offers a structured place to slow clinical decisions down, work through realistic situations, examine the reasoning behind different responses, and build a process you can continue refining with experience and supervision.

Training and Background

Clinical foundation

I’m a Licensed Professional Counselor with a Master’s in Mental Health Counseling and experience across multiple levels of mental health care.

My clinical background keeps everything I teach grounded in ethical practice, responsible decision making, and the realities of client care.

Mind body training

I have trained in yoga, mindfulness, somatic work, and movement based practices.

This background informs how I understand stress, attention, and the body’s response to pressure.

Creative movement

Movement has been both a profession and a laboratory for understanding how people learn challenging skills.

Working across different disciplines, skill levels, and performance environments taught me how to break complex goals into observable parts, adjust instruction in real time, and offer feedback people can actually use.

Outside of The Confident Clinician, I run a private therapy practice for women who are tired of holding everything together while quietly falling apart. The anxiety. The sadness. The burnout. The "I should have it more together by now" feeling.

The work I do combines evidence based talk therapy with body based and mindfulness informed approaches because understanding the nervous system intellectually is not the same as actually learning to regulate it.

You are not sitting across from a wall of credentials. You are sitting with someone who has done their own work, stays curious, and brings as much of themselves into the room as they ask you to.
