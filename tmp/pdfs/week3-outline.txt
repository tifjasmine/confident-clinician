The  Job  of  Week  3  
By  Week  3,  participants  have  learned:  
●  why  confidence  feels  shaky,  
---PAGE 356---
●  how  therapists  develop,  ●  how  to  regulate  themselves,  ●  and  how  to  think  through  a  clinical  moment  using  CLEAR.  
Now  they  face  a  new  challenge.  
They  understand  the  client.  
They  understand  the  moment.  
But  they  still  wonder:  
"How  do  I  actually  lead  the  session?"  
Many  newer  therapists  assume  there  are  only  two  options.  
Either...  
●  let  the  client  talk  wherever  they  want  and  hope  therapy  eventually  happens,  
or...  
●  become  rigid,  overly  structured,  and  accidentally  sound  like  a  therapy  robot.  
Week  3  teaches  the  third  option:  
Collaborative  clinical  leadership.  
The  therapist  learns  how  to  create  movement  without  taking  over.  
How  to  provide  direction  without  removing  choice.  
How  to  guide  the  work  without  performing  therapy.

What  This  Week  Is  Really  Teaching  
This  is  not  a  week  about  scripts.  
It  is  not  about  memorizing  openings.  
It  is  not  about  controlling  clients.  
It  is  not  about  making  every  session  emotional.  
It  is  about  learning  one  core  skill:  
Helping  fifty  minutes  of  human  conversation  become  intentional  clinical  work.  
 
What  This  Week  Must  NOT  Become  
●  A  collection  of  therapist  one-liners  ●  Fifty  conversation  scripts  ●  A  rigid  beginning-middle-end  formula  ●  "Here's  exactly  what  to  say."  ●  One  perfect  session  structure  ●  A  promise  that  every  session  should  create  a  breakthrough  
---PAGE 358---
●  Advice  that  ignores  readiness,  culture,  diagnosis,  context,  or  risk  
Instead...  
this  week  teaches  participants  how  to  think  about  leading  a  session.  
 
VIDEO  1  
Structure  Is  Not  Control  
Why  Good  Sessions  Need  a  Spine  
Central  Question  
How  do  I  lead  without  taking  over?  
 
Core  Lesson  
Many  therapists  believe  they  have  two  choices.  
Passive.  
Or  controlling.  
But  therapy  isn't  either.  
Great  therapists  provide  structure  without  removing  collaboration.  
They  don't  force  direction.  
They  create  enough  direction  that  meaningful  work  can  happen.  
Clients  often  don't  need  someone  to  decide  everything.  
They  need  someone  who  can  help  organize  complexity.  
 
---PAGE 359---
Key  Insights  
●  Passivity  is  not  the  same  as  being  client-centered.  ●  Clients  are  often  overwhelmed  by  everything  they  carry.  ●  Structure  creates  freedom—not  restriction.  ●  Leadership  is  different  from  control.  ●  Good  therapists  hold  the  frame  while  remaining  flexible.  ●  Direction  reduces  therapist  anxiety  and  client  overwhelm.  ●  Sessions  don't  need  to  be  perfectly  linear  to  remain  purposeful.  
 
Framework  
Passive  →  Controlling  →  Collaborative  Leadership  
Passive  
"Wherever  you  go,  I'll  follow."  
↓  
Controlling  
"I'll  decide  where  we're  going."  
↓  
Collaborative  Leadership  
"Let's  decide  together  where  this  session  will  be  most  useful."  
 
Tool  
Leadership  Reflection  
Where  do  I  become  passive?  
Where  do  I  become  controlling?  
What  am  I  afraid  will  happen  if  I  lead?  
What  would  collaborative  leadership  look  like?  
---PAGE 360---
 
Core  Takeaway  
Structure  isn't  taking  over  the  session.  It's  helping  the  session  hold  
something.
 
 
VIDEO  2  
The  First  Ten  Minutes  Matter  
Stop  Waiting  for  the  Session  to  Reveal  What  It's  About  
Central  Question  
How  do  I  open  sessions  with  direction  instead  of  hoping  one  appears?  
 
Core  Lesson  
Most  therapists  open  with...  
"So...  
how  have  you  been?"  
Or...  
"What  do  you  want  to  talk  about  today?"  
Those  aren't  bad  questions.  
They're  just  incomplete.  
The  opening  isn't  simply  gathering  updates.  
It's  helping  both  therapist  and  client  answer:  
"What  are  we  using  today's  session  for?"  
---PAGE 361---
 
Key  Insights  
●  Updates  are  not  treatment.  ●  The  opening  creates  orientation.  ●  Good  openings  reduce  scattered  sessions.  ●  Treatment  goals  should  quietly  guide—not  dominate.  ●  Urgent  concerns  should  be  addressed  before  routine  work.  ●  Collaboration  starts  immediately.  
 
Framework  
Connect  
↓  
Update  
↓  
Orient  
↓  
Focus  
Connect  
How  is  the  client  arriving?  
Update  
What  changed?  
Orient  
Is  there  anything  urgent?  
Focus  
What  would  make  today  useful?

Central  Question  
How  do  I  naturally  move  from  storytelling  into  therapeutic  work?  
 
Core  Lesson  
Listening  matters.  
Stories  matter.  
But  eventually...  
the  therapist  has  to  gently  guide  the  client  toward  the  work  hidden  inside  the  story.  
That  transition  is  the  pivot.  
 
Key  Insights  
●  Storytelling  isn't  wasted  time.  ●  Don't  interrupt  too  early.  ●  Don't  wait  forever.  ●  Summaries  create  movement.  ●  Naming  patterns  creates  direction.  ●  Invitations  are  stronger  than  commands.

VIDEO  7  
Create  Movement  Without  Forcing  a  
Breakthrough
 
Understanding  What  Progress  Actually  Looks  Like  
Central  Question  
How  do  I  know  whether  enough  happened  today?  
 
Core  Lesson  
One  of  the  biggest  myths  in  therapy  is  that  every  session  should  end  with  a  breakthrough.  
Sometimes  the  most  important  progress  is  much  quieter.  
Therapy  doesn't  always  move  dramatically.  
It  moves  intentionally.  
 
Key  Insights  
Movement  may  look  like:  
Clarification  
---PAGE 371---
"I  finally  understand  what  keeps  happening."  
Emotional  Contact  
"I  let  myself  actually  feel  it."  
Pattern  Recognition  
"I  see  what  keeps  repeating."  
Relational  Movement  
"I  said  something  honestly."  
Behavioral  Practice  
"I  tried  something  different."  
Decision  Movement  
"I  know  my  next  step."  
Containment  
"I  don't  feel  so  overwhelmed."  
Integration  
"I  can  connect  this  to  what  we're  working  toward."  
 
Framework  
Stay  
↓  
Deepen  
↓  
Practice  
↓  
Integrate
